## What it is

A static site — 34 pages, no WordPress, no database, no PHP. Everything is generated
from plain text files by one Python script and served as flat HTML, so there is nothing
to hack through a plugin and nothing that can go down because a database is busy.

The homepage follows the order in the plan I sent you: the hotel first (key facts and a
booking button), then the date search, the short film, the three room types, the airport
table, then the neighbourhood sections, FAQ and a final booking card. Underneath that
sit the deeper pages — a full facility guide for the hotel, the area articles, airport
routes, and the comparison pieces.

One change from the plan, and it matters. I checked the trademark register (J-PlatPat)
before building. 「HOTEL MERCURY／ホテル マーキュリー」 is a live registered mark for
lodging, held by another company. So the site never presents itself as the hotel: the
masthead, the page titles and the structured data are all the guide's, and the hotel
appears as a subject we write about, in the third person, with a note on every relevant
page that we are independent and that the mark belongs to its owner. The hotel's old
phone, fax, email and the previous operator's booking engine are gone entirely. That
separation is also the thing that makes ranking possible — the old site was competing
with the hotel's own live site for its own brand name, which is a fight it could never
win.

## How it is checked

Every figure on the site — minutes, room sizes, room counts, prices — comes from a
registry of sourced facts and prints with a footnote to where it came from (the hotel's
own site, its booking page, the railway operators, the event organisers). The build
literally refuses to compile if someone types a number with a unit straight into the
text. That was deliberate: on a site like this, one invented detail is the whole
credibility gone.

On top of that, each build checks that no page claims to be the hotel, that every
booking link is labelled as advertising and carries the right tags for Japan's
ステマ規制 rules, that no page is orphaned or links to a dead one, that the structured
data is valid, and that no two pages chase the same search term. I also ran a
colour-contrast pass across every page — all text passes the accessibility floor.

## WordPress

Not attached, and deliberately so. The blog you see is static, same as the rest of the
site, which is why it is fast and needs no maintenance. If you want a WordPress blog on
top of it later, that is its own build — it needs a PHP pool, a database, and a
maintenance routine — and it should happen after the site is moved onto our server, not
before. I would also let the static pages earn some trust first before adding volume;
adding a big blog to a freshly cleaned domain is how the last one got flagged.
