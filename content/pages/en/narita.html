Spotting the through-train

At the airport, read the destination. A Keisei train bound for Oshiage, Asakusa, Nishi-magome or Haneda Airport is running onto the Toei Asakusa Line, and it calls at Asakusabashi. A train terminating at Keisei-Ueno is on Keisei's own line and does not.

The property covered on this site gives {{fact hotel-mercury.time_narita_en}} from Narita as its own estimate for the direct route.

If you take the Skyliner

Ride to Keisei-Ueno, then change to the JR Yamanote or Keihin-Tohoku Line at Ueno, and change again at Akihabara for the JR Sobu Line to Asakusabashi — or take a taxi for the last part. It is quick but involves moving luggage twice, which is the reason many people with big bags take the slower through-train instead.

Arriving at night

Late in the evening, through-services thin out and a train that stops short means an unplanned transfer. Before you book, confirm the last train that reaches Asakusabashi and the hotel's check-in cut-off — the property covered here lists {{fact hotel-mercury.checkin_en}}. The Toei timetable for the airport direction from Asakusabashi is published by the operator{{cite toei-a16-timetable}}.
