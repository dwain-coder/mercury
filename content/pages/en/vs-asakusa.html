The short answer

Stay in Asakusa if the temple district is the reason you came and you want to step out of the front door into it. Stay in Asakusabashi if you plan to move around Tokyo, or if you are arriving from or leaving for an airport and would rather not change trains.

What staying in Asakusa is like

You wake up inside the sight. Sensoji before the crowds arrive is the single best argument for it. The trade-off is that the district is busy from mid-morning until evening, and that getting to Akihabara, Shinjuku or Tokyo Station means at least one change.

{{photo kaminarimon ratio="16/9"}}

What staying in Asakusabashi is like

A neighbourhood that works for a living: Japanese doll makers with decades behind them, wholesalers selling packaging and shop fittings, delivery vans in the morning{{cite kyugetsu}}{{cite shimojima}}. Little is aimed at visitors, which is the point. The Sumida River is a short walk east, at Yanagibashi — {{walk br-yanagibashi}} from the hotel covered here.

{{photo yanagibashi-day ratio="16/9"}}

From the station you have two lines in two useful directions: JR Sobu west to Akihabara and Shinjuku, Toei Asakusa north-east to Asakusa and Oshiage and south to Nihombashi and Ginza.

Decide by your itinerary

  • Two days, mostly around the temple → Asakusa.
  • Several days, moving across the city → Asakusabashi.
  • An early flight, or a late landing → Asakusabashi, for the short walk from a station with airport through-trains ({{fact hotel-mercury.access_toei_en}}).
  • Travelling as three or more → check room capacity wherever you look; the property covered here lists {{fact hotel-mercury.room_breakdown_en}}.
