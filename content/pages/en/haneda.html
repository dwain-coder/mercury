How the route works

Haneda Airport's station is on the Keikyu Line. Keikyu tracks connect to the Toei Asakusa Line, and many trains run straight through from one to the other{{cite keikyu}}{{cite toei-subway}}. A through-train passes Sengakuji, continues under central Tokyo and calls at Asakusabashi on its way towards Asakusa and Oshiage.

Reading the destination board

On the airport platform, look at where the train is going, not at the line name.

  • Destination Oshiage, Asakusa, or anywhere on the Keisei Line → it runs through the Toei Asakusa Line, so Asakusabashi is direct.
  • Destination Shinagawa → it stops short of the Toei line; you change there.

Going the other way, from Asakusabashi to the airport, use the Toei Asakusa Line platform signed for Nishi-magome, Haneda Airport and the Keikyu Line, and take a train bound for Haneda Airport{{cite toei-a16-timetable}}.

Journey time

The hotel covered on this site gives {{fact hotel-mercury.time_haneda_en}} from Haneda as its own estimate. Treat that as a planning figure and check a journey planner on the day — train types differ and late-evening services thin out.

Arriving at night

If you may arrive after the cut-off, tell the property when you book, or choose somewhere with a later desk. There is an interactive version of this calculation on the Japanese page for late arrivals.

When you get there

Asakusabashi station has two lines under one name: JR Sobu for Akihabara and Shinjuku, Toei Asakusa for Asakusa, Nihombashi and Oshiage. The hotel covered on this site is {{fact hotel-mercury.access_toei_en}}. Luggage: {{fact hotel-mercury.luggage_en}}.
